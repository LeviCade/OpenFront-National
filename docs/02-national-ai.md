# 02 — National-interest AI and strategic memory

## The objective

**CORE RULE:** Every independent NATIONAL nation seeks **Sovereign Victory** or **Imperial Victory** if it becomes imperial ruler. A constituent state seeks an **Empire Victory** with the same strategic urgency. No AI nation is an anti-human antagonist by design.

Do not mistake "maximize immediate safety" for "maximize probability of winning". Sometimes a decisive existential war is wiser than waiting to be conquered. Equally, do not treat fighting a runaway leader as inherently desirable.

## Unified strategic planner

A nation periodically maintains a strategic state describing:

- Its political identity (independent, imperial sovereign, constituent), permitted actions and winning outcome.
- Geographic expansion routes, bordering rivals, distant powers, maritime reach, terrain and defensive viability.
- Military posture: current and projected force, reserves, vulnerability to retaliation, naval transport, strongholds, assets, active wars, potential coalitions.
- Economic options: investment, commerce, ports, trade exposure, embargoes, taxes, ability to sustain military campaigns.
- Diplomatic opportunities: alliance offers, treaty obligations, imperial accessions, coercive demands, sanctions, peace.
- Current primary objective and shorter-range tactical subgoals.
- Credible future risks and opportunities under incomplete information.

The nation should choose a _strategy_ rather than merely apply isolated, conflicting attack/donation/alliance heuristics. Tactical systems act inside that strategy and may override it when immediate defense is necessary.

## Examples of admissible strategies

Expand into weak neighbors; consolidate before a major offensive; protect critical ports; become an opportunistic ally to a distant giant; resist a credible annexation ultimatum; exploit a giant engaged overseas; impose secondary sanctions instead of initiating hopeless war; become an empire's member as a last-resort existential choice; or join defensive agreements to slow a threatening rival.

No forced balance-of-power script. Choosing not to join a collectively beneficial coalition is allowed.

## National memory and credibility

Remember concrete observed interactions: treaty acceptance and betrayal, failed commitments, fulfilled threats, war outcomes, defensive levies, sanctions compliance, diplomatic proposals, imperial transfers, and relations. Observations should update estimates of willingness to follow through, reliability, likely objectives, and hostility.

A repeatedly bluffing superpower may lose its leverage; a smaller power with a proven record of enforcing sanctions may be taken seriously. Memory cannot reveal information that was never observed legitimately.

## Information fairness

The planner must access an **observation view**, not unrestricted authoritative state. Human and AI nation identities are gameplay-neutral. The planner sees only visible troops, structures, ships, public stats, legitimate coalition stats, intelligence reports, and national history. Hidden treaty constitutions and unrevealed enemy positions are unavailable.

No clairvoyant opponent counters, no unearned detection of transports' landing targets, and no free ability to price hidden war risk perfectly.

## Imperial state behavior

Before accession, a nation regards surrender of supreme victory as a major loss of sovereignty and prestige, even if member victory is a legitimate win. Joining is not a generic "my estimated win rate is low" choice. An empire's trajectory, geographic ability to protect/coerce, reliability, and actual threat of elimination matter.

After accession, work full-force for imperial victory and comply with imperial policy, including delegated freedom, military levies, taxes, centralization and external commitments.

The Imperial Nation optimizes the entire empire without inventing assets or information. Member intelligence is shared across the empire according to the intelligence specification.

## Planning cadence and compute

Do substantial strategic planning periodically and on meaningful events (war declared, member attacked, ultimatum issued, empire collapse, loss of key infrastructure, new intelligence, peace concluded). Avoid exhaustive reevaluation every simulation tick. Keep deterministic randomness and stable action ordering.

## AI acceptance examples

- Same geopolitical position with a human opponent versus AI opponent produces the same strategic analysis, ignoring identity.
- Distant oversized leader does not automatically override a local profitable expansion.
- Small nation may accept favorable defense pact with giant, rather than reject purely for size.
- Nation may refuse a massive but geographically incapable empire's ultimatum.
- National AI cannot know invisible warships or hidden treaty levy terms.
- Member state expends full available strategic effort for imperial victory.
- Nations evaluate whether a sanctions threat costs the issuer more than following through is worth.

Implementation parameters, planning score functions, and personality distribution remain **OPEN**, constrained by these behavioral requirements.
