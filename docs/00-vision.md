# 00 — Vision and non-negotiable principles

## Mission

OpenFront: NATIONAL — **The Age of Sovereignty** — is a proposed next-level Solo FFA experience in which independent nations and empires compete for the final sovereign victory. Each political entity pursues its own interests, forms or rejects treaties, remembers history, assesses threats, and can use diplomacy, military power, economics, and intelligence to shape an emergent world.

**The golden rule:** Every nation wants to **WIN**. A nation does not exist to dogpile a human, balance the map, or altruistically sacrifice itself for another country's benefit.

## The seven design laws

1. **National interest comes first.** Decisions seek the best credible path to victory based on the nation's present capabilities, future opportunities, geographic realities, and survival risk. A distant superpower is not necessarily an immediate threat. A nearby exploitable rival might matter more.
2. **Sovereignty matters.** Independent victory and serving as the imperial sovereign are equally prestigious supreme victories. Being an ordinary member of a victorious empire is a lesser but legitimate victory. Independent nations resist surrendering sovereignty until a concrete existential crisis and lack of alternatives make accession rational; pure relative statistical weakness is not enough.
3. **A member state fights to win.** Upon joining an empire, a constituent nation works just as hard and existentially for its empire's victory as an independent country works for its own. It doesn't intentionally sabotage or coast because its victory receives lesser prestige.
4. **No omniscient or privileged AI.** All NATIONAL AI evaluates humans and AI nations identically. No identity-driven aggression, free resources, hidden troop cheats, or access to information hidden from that nation. Better decisions, not different rules.
5. **Authority, scope, enforcement.** Every diplomatic and military act specifies who can authorize it, whom it binds, what state changes, and what happens on breach, default, cancellation, or collapse. Do not rely on unenforceable promises masquerading as game mechanics.
6. **Consequences emerge from incentives.** Coalitions can form, overlap, betray, or fail; limited national wars may run beneath wider imperial wars. Coercive demands succeed only when credible. Self-interest can produce a disastrous failure to coordinate; that's part of the game.
7. **Preserve OpenFront's immediacy.** Despite deep systems, keep the playable core real-time, legible, efficient, and decisive. Automation and sensible policy defaults take priority over requiring constant modal micromanagement.

## A strategic world, not an anti-leader script

A group of ten 200K nations may collectively defeat a 1M nation but individual members risk elimination. An individual AI should examine who bears retaliation, which allies will follow through, and whether intervening improves its *own* chance of surviving and ultimately winning. The most rational move may be to consolidate, free-ride, align with the giant, attack when the giant is overcommitted, or fight an existential war. NATIONAL permits all outcomes.

No rule forces nations to attack the largest player simply because its land share exceeds a difficulty threshold. The "crown" threat becomes one input to a larger national-interest assessment.

## The geopolitical framework

- **Independent nations** possess external sovereignty. They may join multiple ordinary coalitions and decide whether to submit to one exclusive empire.
- **Non-Aggression Pacts** create constitutional promises not to fight.
- **Mutual Defense Alliances** can automatically requisition defensively committed troops under explicit levy rules.
- **Empires** comprise one Imperial Nation and subordinate member nations under one supreme sovereign with customizable internal governance and military administration.
- **War and peace** are formal, scoped states. Economic embargoes can exist in peacetime. Piracy requires an actual war. National wars and empire-wide wars remain separate records.
- **Intelligence** is limited by location, state, pacts, and intelligence investments; hidden rules stay hidden from outsiders.
- **Victory** requires the final sovereign unit (one nation or one empire) to be the only one left, not just dominance by land percentage.

## What NATIONAL is not

- Not a claim that these mechanics already exist upstream.
- Not a replacement for every existing OpenFront difficulty or multiplayer mode. Legacy functionality remains until an explicitly tested migration strategy says otherwise.
- Not an economic total-conversion. Currencies, goods, sovereign credit, collateral, construction repricing, and real supply chains are **deferred**.
- Not an upstream-endorsed official game release.

## Design quality bar

Before accepting a mechanic, ask: Does it produce an interesting, self-interested choice? Is the cost borne by a real actor? Can a rational opponent counter it? Are its implications for war, empire policy, secrecy, and victory explicit? Can the engine prove it in a deterministic test?

The desired experience is **fast territorial strategy with extraordinary political depth**, not procedural complexity for its own sake.
