# 06 — Embargoes, secondary sanctions, coercion, and blockades

## Trade and war are distinct

**CORE RULE:** A nation may prohibit trade with another nation **while at peace**. An embargo is an economic permission, not a declaration of war. Piracy of a trade ship requires an applicable formal war between its attacking authority and the ship's owning nation.

A valid war automatically prohibits direct trade between its belligerents. A pre-existing peacetime embargo persists if peace ends but the embargo has not been rescinded.

Empire-level trade policies may apply broadly or specifically: outside X versus all of empire E; X versus member B only; member B versus member C. The emperor may override local member trade policies. Member-specific wars do not automatically prohibit neutral imperial members trading the same foreign nation.

## Secondary sanctions

An independent power A can issue a formal public or targeted demand: "Nations designated by this declaration must cease trade with B; continuing trade with B will cause A to embargo you." The demand specifies issuer, target B, recipient scope, deadline, trigger, and threatened embargo.

It **cannot magically command** recipient nations to comply. They independently assess their revenue ties to A and B, substitutes, military/financial prospects, reputation, and likelihood A follows through.

At expiry or detection of noncompliance, A must either implement its declared embargoes, negotiate, or visibly back down. Backing down affects *observed credibility*; no automatic financial penalty or omniscient reputation is necessary. No war is required for ordinary sanctions. Sanctions by themselves never authorize piracy or attack.

**Imperial sanctions:** Imperial Nation may issue a sweeping secondary sanction backed by the collective trading power of all members. A subordinate can propose but **cannot issue an empire-binding secondary sanction without the emperor's approval**. External sanctions demands may be handled locally by permitted imperial members or centrally by emperor policy. External calls for one imperial member to embargo another default to rejection; the emperor may override on internal policy grounds.

**Anti-abuse:** Deterministic sanctions state transitions; no automatic cascade that creates infinite sanction declarations; measured compliance using actual trade permissions and actual transactions; no hidden free economic damage.

## Coercive imperial ultimatums

An Imperial Nation may demand that a sovereign outsider **join the empire**, warning of specific consequences such as peacetime embargo, secondary sanctions, a scoped war, military invasion or empire-wide war. A nation may also independently request empire admission when facing catastrophe.

A demand is not a forced annexation. The target must expressly accept accession or refuse; a threat merely raises the cost of refusal. The AI must assess **enforceability**, not just relative land share: borders, sea reach, warships/transport capability, geography, SAM defenses, economic costs, available defense allies, and how likely the emperor is to carry out its threat.

Demands have deadlines; acceptance, refusal, withdrawal or expiry are distinct events. A failed bluff can diminish future credibility. An empire deep in decline may be unattractive even to a threatened outsider. No automatic "small nation joins big empire" script.

## Piracy and member-specific proxy wars

A pirate may target a trade ship only if the warship's **actual controlling belligerent** is at war with the ship owner. This applies to independent, imperial, and member-specific war scopes.

An emperor may fund a small constituent state, delegate a national war against outside trading partners, and authorize that state's warships to pirate wartime enemies while the rest of the empire remains formally at peace. This is a legal emergent strategy under the rules. The outside world may retaliate against that member, embargo the larger empire, or itself declare an empire-wide war. The empire does not gain consequence-free immunity.

Funding and warships are real assets; imperial troop/treasury policy governs transfers. An imperial war, once declared, automatically makes all members belligerents.

## Physically enforced maritime blockades

This is **not** an arbitrary passive income debuff. A nation at war with target X may use actual warships near X's ports or maritime approaches to blockade X's maritime trade routes. Zone radius and path-interception details are **OPEN** balance/engineering choices.

When a valid blockading warship meets an eligible ship trying to access the blockaded port/route:
- **Enemy ship** (owning nation at war with blockading power): normal wartime capture/piracy behavior.
- **Neutral nation's trade ship**: it is **not captured**. It is turned around toward its nearest eligible home port; on return it yields **no trade income** to either party from that failed journey.
- **Ship unrelated to restricted destination or route**: unaffected.

Blockade requires physical warship presence and an active war against the nation being blockaded. Neutral shipping is not pirated merely for attempting trade. Destroying or displacing the blockading force breaks enforcement. No magical worldwide blockade at unlimited range.

**OPEN:** precise zone geometry; reroute/return during port loss, border changes, simultaneous interception; whether a neutral may choose a different eligible destination instead of returning; anti-loop rules to prevent an interception of an already-returning vessel.

## Future peace consideration

Conditional peace involving payments, minimum peace periods, collateral, and territorial concessions is **DEFERRED**. Simple formal peace by two-party acceptance is CORE. A concession that can immediately be reversed by a fresh war is not meaningful unless enforcement is explicitly defined. No complicated finance needed for NATIONAL v1.

## Acceptance

- Embargo without war blocks trade but not piracy permission.
- Pirating a neutral ship fails.
- A wartime blockade returns neutral trade vessels home with zero revenue.
- A blockade ends when enforcing fleet is gone or war terminates.
- Member H may have proxy wars while other empire members remain neutral.
- Secondary sanction target may defy; issuer must enforce or back down; no forced compliance.
- Imperial secondary sanctions bind members only after emperor authorization.
